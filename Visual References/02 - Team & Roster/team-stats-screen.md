# Team Stats Screen Concept

## Role

Team Stats is a customizable analytics workspace for understanding the user’s franchise in both absolute and league-relative terms. It should support a quick review of the team’s current season and deeper comparison with every other team.

## Tile-based analytics layout

The screen uses the same responsive, editable grid model as the Home dashboard. It does not prescribe one fixed analytics view.

- The user chooses which information tiles to display, changes their sizes, and arranges them into a preferred analysis layout.
- Tiles can be tables, direct comparisons, league rankings, charts/graphs, or other useful data representations.
- The default layout provides a useful starting point, but users can build a view around offense, defense, special teams, situational performance, or any personal evaluation preference.
- Tiles adapt their detail to their size and open an appropriate full comparison/detail view when selected.

## Available information

The available tile catalog can cover straightforward team statistics and league context, including:

- Offensive, defensive, and special-teams totals and rates.
- Direct comparison of the user’s team against league average, leaders, selected teams, division rivals, or the entire league.
- League rankings for any supported category.
- Trend graphs across games, weeks, or seasons.
- Situational and split views when the simulation supports them (for example, home/away, recent form, or phase of season).
- Player contribution summaries that connect team outcomes to the roster.

## Interaction and presentation

- Every comparison must clearly identify its scope, timeframe, and units so charts do not create ambiguous conclusions.
- Table tiles preserve the dense, sortable FM/OOTP working feel; visual tiles use restrained, readable charts to reveal trends or relative standing at a glance.
- Selecting a team, category, player, or rank leads naturally to the relevant deeper screen.
- Filters for season, timeframe, and comparison group should be shared where appropriate, while individual tiles can retain a purposeful local setting.

## Visual direction

Data remains the focus. Charts and ranking treatments earn their space only when they make a relationship easier to understand than a table; they are not decorative filler. Team branding can identify the user’s franchise and selected comparisons, but league-wide views must stay neutral and highly legible.
